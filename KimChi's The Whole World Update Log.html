About The Wildlife

Recent updates in the animal world feature groundbreaking cloning successes for endangered mammals and exciting new species discoveries.

Conservation and Cloning Breakthroughs

  • Cloning Endangered Species: Scientists recently reported success in cloning an endangered Indian guar and are now working to clone the rare red-striped African bongo using surrogate eland antelopes.

  • Saving Jaguars: Conservationists in Argentina are expanding rewilding projects by moving jaguars to new areas and planning safe wildlife corridors across South America.

  • Wolf Protections: Officials in the United States are reviewing potential rule changes to adjust endangered species protections for gray wolves.

Global Conflicts

Major international updates include expanding conflict in the Middle East, renewed missile strikes in Ukraine, and new retaliatory tariffs between Canada and the United States.

Middle East Conflict

  • Houthi Attacks: Iran-backed Houthi forces launched strikes on energy facilities in Saudi Arabia. More than 70 people were wounded.

  • The conflict between the United States and Iran remains at a six-month stalemate. Oil prices near $100 a barrel as shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz slows down.

Russia and Ukraine

  • Kyiv Strikes: Russian drone and missile attacks hit Kyiv, killing multiple people.

  • Talks Paused: The renewed attacks end a brief pause that coincided with a visit by United States peace envoys

Global Trade

  • Canada Tariffs: Canada’s retaliatory tariffs against the United States officially went into effect. Trade talks between the two nations remain stalled.

Climate Change

A new United Nations report shows the world will pass the 1.5°C global warming limit soon, but countries can still adapt and lower temperatures later

Global Warming Thresholds

  • Passing 1.5°C: A recent report from the UNEP states the world is set to miss the Paris Agreement target.

  • Peak Temperatures: Global temperatures are expected to pass 1.5°C soon and could reach 1.8°C by 2050.

  • New Goal: Scientists suggest an "overshoot, peak, and decline" plan to bring temperatures back down over time.
